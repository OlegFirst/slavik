## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This document extracts actionable business continuity flows specific to healthcare systems from WHO guidance documents. These flows complement and extend beyond ISO 22301 standard requirements by addressing healthcare-specific operational realities including:

- **Patient-centered continuity** (vs. general business operations)
- **Clinical service prioritization** during emergencies
- **Healthcare workforce resilience** (unique vulnerabilities)
- **Medical supply chain criticality**
- **Vulnerable population protection**
- **Disease-specific program continuity** (HIV, TB, etc.)

## SECTION 2: HEALTHCARE-SPECIFIC BCM FLOWS

### FLOW 1: Health Service Continuity Planning Lifecycle

**Purpose:** Continuous process to maintain essential health services during public health emergencies

**Steps:**

1. **Form Collaborative Planning Team**
   - **Input:** Health facility leadership decision
   - **Process:**
     - Establish planning team with clear terms of reference
     - Include: facility departments + external stakeholders
     - Define roles: head of facility, emergency response team, department heads
     - Engage: community representatives, other facilities, health authorities, WHO, partners, non-health sectors
   - **Output:** Planning team with documented ToR and contact list
   - **Timeline:** Initial setup 2-4 weeks

2. **Conduct Risk Assessment and Prioritize Risks**
   - **Input:** Planning team established
   - **Process:**
     - Identify internal and external hazards
     - Assess: likelihood, preparedness level, impact on staff/premises/services/population
     - Consider secondary risks (financial difficulties, additional outbreaks, social unrest)
     - Develop facility risk register
   - **Output:** Prioritized risk list with impact analysis
   - **Tools:** WHO risk assessment matrix, hazard vulnerability assessment tools
   - **Timeline:** 4-6 weeks

3. **Determine Overall Objectives and Operational Priorities**
   - **Input:** Risk assessment results
   - **Process:**
     - Define desired end-state during/after emergencies
     - Categorize services:
       * Essential (must maintain)
       * Relocatable (to other facilities/platforms)
       * Temporarily suspendable
     - Prioritize based on local demography and epidemiology
   - **Output:** Service prioritization matrix aligned with essential health services package
   - **Timeline:** 2-3 weeks

4. **Conduct Capacity Assessment**
   - **Input:** Operational priorities defined
   - **Process:**
     - Map available resources: health workers, infrastructure, bed capacity, medicines, equipment, IT, funding
     - Identify required resources for essential services
     - Account for resources diverted to emergency response
     - Identify surge capacity options
     - Determine resource gaps
   - **Output:** Resource gap analysis with mobilization plan
   - **Timeline:** 3-4 weeks

5. **Develop Service Continuity Plan**
   - **Input:** Risk assessment + capacity assessment + priorities
   - **Process:**
     - Establish activation/deactivation triggers
     - Define worst-case scenario assumptions
     - Identify activities for each programming area
     - Assign responsibilities and timelines
     - Document procedures clearly and simply
     - Align with national/subnational emergency plans
   - **Output:** Written service continuity plan with annexes
   - **Format:** Template in Section 5 of WHO Handbook
   - **Timeline:** 6-8 weeks

6. **Test and Update Plan**
   - **Input:** Completed plan
   - **Process:**
     - Conduct simulation exercises
     - Test activation procedures
     - Verify communication pathways
     - Train staff on procedures
     - Document lessons learned
     - Update plan based on findings
   - **Output:** Tested and refined plan with training records
   - **Frequency:** Minimum annually or when risks change
   - **Timeline:** Ongoing

7. **Implement Plan and Monitor Implementation**
   - **Input:** Trigger event occurs
   - **Process:**
     - Activate plan per predetermined triggers
     - Mobilize stakeholders and resources
     - Monitor implementation continuously
     - Adapt as situation evolves
     - Track key performance indicators
   - **Output:** Maintained essential services + implementation logs
   - **Timeline:** Duration of emergency

8. **Conduct Post-Event Review and Update Plan**
   - **Input:** Emergency ends or plan deactivated
   - **Process:**
     - Conduct After-Action Review (AAR)
     - Document successes and failures
     - Identify immediate, medium, and long-term improvements
     - Update plan based on lessons learned
     - Share lessons with stakeholders
   - **Output:** Improved plan + lessons learned documentation
   - **Timeline:** Within 1-2 months post-event

**Dependencies:**
- Senior management support and funding
- Stakeholder engagement across sectors
- Access to risk and capacity data
- Alignment with national plans

**Success Criteria:**
- All 8 steps completed and documented
- Plan tested through exercises
- Staff trained and aware
- Resources identified and accessible

### FLOW 2: All-Hazards Service Continuity Approach

**Purpose:** Unified planning framework adaptable to multiple hazard types rather than siloed plans

**Healthcare-Specific Context:**
The all-hazards approach in healthcare recognizes common needs across diverse emergencies while allowing hazard-specific adaptations:

**Common Elements Across Hazards:**

1. **Service Prioritization Needs**
   - Maintain life-saving services
   - Protect vulnerable populations
   - Ensure staff safety
   - Maintain supply chains

2. **Resource Mobilization Patterns**
   - Surge capacity activation
   - Mutual aid agreements
   - Alternative service delivery sites
   - Emergency procurement

3. **Communication Requirements**
   - Risk communication to public
   - Coordination with authorities
   - Internal staff communication
   - Inter-facility collaboration

**Hazard-Specific Adaptations:**

| Hazard Type | Specific Considerations |
|-------------|------------------------|
| **Infectious Disease Outbreaks** | - Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures<br>- Isolation spaces<br>- Contact tracing<br>- Vaccination campaigns<br>- Testing capacity |
| **Natural Disasters (Earthquakes, Floods)** | - Structural safety assessment<br>- Alternative facility sites<br>- Mass casualty management<br>- Evacuation procedures |
| **Chemical/Radiological Events** | - Decontamination procedures<br>- Toxicology expertise<br>- Protective equipment<br>- Specialized treatment protocols |
| **Armed Conflict/Violence** | - Security measures<br>- Staff protection<br>- Refugee/displaced population services<br>- Trauma care surge |
| **Cybersecurity Incidents** | - Manual backup systems<br>- Data protection<br>- Communication alternatives<br>- IT recovery procedures |

**Process Flow:**

1. **Baseline Planning (All-Hazards)**
   - Identify critical health services to maintain
   - Establish core resource requirements
   - Define essential staffing levels
   - Map alternative delivery sites
   - Create communication frameworks

2. **Hazard-Specific Annexes**
   - Develop supplementary procedures for priority hazards
   - Include hazard-specific equipment/supplies
   - Define specialized expertise needed
   - Document unique protocols

3. **Risk-Based Prioritization**
   - Focus detailed planning on highest-risk hazards
   - Maintain flexibility for unexpected events
   - Regular risk reassessment
   - Update priorities as context changes

**Advantages for Healthcare:**
- Cost-effective (avoid duplicate planning)
- Efficient resource use
- Clear common procedures
- Flexible adaptation
- Simplified training

**WHO-Specific Guidance:**
> "The all-hazards approach does not imply that authorities should prepare for all hazards; rather, it focuses on common needs that emerge from various types of emergencies. Although the all-hazards planning approach can provide a basic framework, service continuity planners should prioritize preparedness for hazards that pose a higher risk for the facility and services based on local, dynamic risk assessment."

### FLOW 3: Health System Building Blocks Integration

**Purpose:** Ensure service continuity planning addresses all dimensions of health system functionality

**WHO Framework Adaptation:**

Healthcare business continuity must address 6+ building blocks:

#### 3.1 LEADERSHIP AND GOVERNANCE

**Continuity Actions:**

**Pre-Emergency:**
- Establish incident management system
- Define clear chain of command
- Create coordination structures with partners
- Develop mutual aid agreements (public/private facilities)
- Secure senior management commitment
- Allocate budget for planning

**During Emergency:**
- Activate incident command structure
- Coordinate with local/national health authorities
- Implement plan oversight
- Make timely decisions based on situation reports
- Manage public-private partnerships

**Post-Emergency:**
- Conduct governance review
- Update coordination mechanisms
- Document decision-making effectiveness
- Improve future coordination

**Healthcare-Specific Considerations:**
- Hospital incident management system (HIMS)
- Multi-facility coordination
- Public health authority interface
- Medical ethics committee engagement during triage decisions

---

#### 3.2 SERVICE DELIVERY

**Continuity Actions:**

**Essential Services Identification:**

```
PRIORITY 1: Life-Saving Services (Cannot be interrupted)
├── Emergency care
├── Critical care (ICU)
├── Labor and delivery
├── Surgical emergencies
├── Dialysis
├── Chemotherapy
└── Antiretroviral therapy (HIV)

PRIORITY 2: Essential Chronic Disease Management
├── Diabetes management
├── Hypertension control
├── TB treatment continuation
├── Mental health crisis services
└── Palliative care

PRIORITY 3: Preventive Services (May be modified)
├── Routine immunizations
├── Antenatal care
├── Well-child visits
└── Cancer screenings

PRIORITY 4: Elective Services (Can be temporarily suspended)
├── Routine surgeries
├── Cosmetic procedures
└── Non-urgent specialist consultations
```

**Service Delivery Modifications:**

| Normal Operations | Emergency Adaptations |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Facility-based outpatient | Home-based care, telemedicine |
| In-person consultations | Remote consultations, phone follow-up |
| Routine scheduling | Priority-based scheduling |
| Standard operating hours | Extended/modified hours |
| Full service menu | Prioritized essential services |

**Alternative Delivery Platforms:**
- Community-based services (CHW-led)
- Temporary field hospitals
- Mobile clinics
- Pharmacy-based distribution (medicines)
- Digital health platforms (telemedicine)

**Quality Maintenance:**
- Continue quality assessment even when services modified
- Monitor patient outcomes
- Track adverse events
- Maintain infection control standards
- Ensure medication safety

#### 3.3 HEALTH WORKFORCE

**Continuity Actions:**

**Pre-Emergency:**
- Identify essential personnel by function
- Create surge staffing roster
- Train staff on emergency procedures
- Establish backup for key positions
- Develop mutual aid agreements with other facilities
- Plan for staff well-being and mental health

**During Emergency:**
- Activate surge capacity staff
- Redeploy staff to priority areas
- Implement staff protection measures (PPE, security)
- Monitor staff health and well-being
- Provide mental health support
- Ensure adequate rest periods

**Post-Emergency:**
- Assess staff impact (illness, trauma, burnout)
- Provide recovery support
- Document lessons on staffing
- Update workforce plans

**Healthcare-Specific Workforce Challenges:**

1. **Clinical Staff Shortages:**
   - Disease outbreak → staff illness
   - Fear of infection → absenteeism
   - Increased workload → burnout
   - Migration during conflict

2. **Specialty Requirements:**
   - Infectious disease specialists during outbreaks
   - Trauma surgeons during conflicts
   - Intensive care specialists during surge
   - Mental health providers for staff/patients

3. **Surge Capacity Sources:**
   - Retired healthcare workers
   - Private sector partnerships
   - Military medical personnel
   - Volunteer medical corps
   - Task-shifting to trained non-clinicians

4. **Retention Strategies:**
   - Hazard pay/incentives
   - Safe working conditions
   - Mental health support
   - Recognition and appreciation
   - Clear communication

#### 3.4 HEALTH FINANCING

**Continuity Actions:**

**Pre-Emergency:**
- Identify funding sources for emergencies
- Pre-allocate emergency budget
- Establish rapid procurement procedures
- Plan for potential loss of user fee revenue
- Create emergency financial protocols

**During Emergency:**
- Release emergency funds
- Remove financial barriers to essential services
- Activate emergency procurement
- Track emergency expenditures
- Seek additional donor funding if needed

**Post-Emergency:**
- Reconcile emergency expenditures
- Assess financial impact on facility
- Secure recovery funding
- Update financial contingency plans

**Healthcare-Specific Financial Issues:**

**Revenue Disruptions:**
- Loss of elective procedure income
- Reduced outpatient visits
- Inability to collect user fees during crisis
- Delayed reimbursements

**Unexpected Costs:**
- Emergency supplies (PPE, medicines)
- Surge staffing expenses
- Alternative facility setup
- Equipment purchases
- Decontamination/cleaning

**Financial Sustainability During Transition:**
- Donor funding withdrawal (e.g., Global Fund transitions)
- Economic crisis reducing government budgets
- Need to maintain essential programs (HIV, TB)
- Building domestic financing resilience

### FLOW 5: Pandemic/Epidemic Response Continuity

**Purpose:** Maintain non-COVID/non-epidemic health services during infectious disease outbreaks

**WHO-Identified Challenge:**
> "COVID-19 has demonstrated that when health is at risk, everything else (e.g., business, trade, tourism, education) is at risk, underscoring the inter-dependency of health and development."

**Pandemic-Specific Service Continuity Issues:**

#### 5.1 Dual Service Burden

**Challenge:** Maintain routine services WHILE responding to pandemic

**Healthcare-Specific Complexity:**

```
SIMULTANEOUS DEMANDS:
┌──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┐
│   PANDEMIC RESPONSE          │   ROUTINE SERVICES           │
├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ COVID testing                │ Chronic disease management   │
│ COVID treatment              │ Maternal/child health        │
│ Isolation ward management    │ HIV/TB treatment             │
│ Contact tracing              │ Immunizations                │
│ Vaccination campaigns        │ Emergency care (non-COVID)   │
│ IPC implementation           │ Mental health services       │
│ Surge staffing               │ Elective surgeries           │
└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
         ↓                                    ↓
    RESOURCE COMPETITION: Staff, Space, Supplies, Funding
```

**Continuity Strategies:**

1. **Service Segregation:**
   - Separate COVID and non-COVID care pathways
   - Dedicated staff teams
   - Physical separation when possible
   - Time-based separation (e.g., COVID clinic mornings, routine afternoons)

2. **Alternative Delivery Modalities:**
   - Home-based care for chronic diseases
   - Telemedicine for consultations
   - Community distribution of medications
   - Drive-through services (testing, vaccination)

3. **Service Modification:**
   - Extended prescription refills (reduce visits)
   - Group visit consolidation (e.g., ANC cohorts)
   - Symptom-based triage for prioritization
   - Defer non-urgent procedures

4. **IPC Integration:**
   - Universal masking
   - Enhanced screening
   - Improved ventilation
   - Hand hygiene stations

#### 5.2 Critical Program Continuity (HIV, TB)

**WHO/EECA Specific Guidance:**

**HIV Service Continuity During Emergencies:**

**Essential Services to Maintain:**
- Antiretroviral therapy (ART) provision
- Prevention services (PrEP, harm reduction)
- Testing and counseling
- Opportunistic infection treatment
- Viral load monitoring

**Continuity Strategies:**

**Multi-Month Dispensing (MMD):**
- Provide 3-6 month ART supplies
- Reduce clinic visit frequency
- Enable clients to weather disruptions

**Differentiated Service Delivery (DSD):**
- Community ART distribution
- Peer-led support groups
- Fast-track refills for stable clients
- Home-based care for advanced disease

**Key Population Protections:**
- Mobile services for hard-to-reach groups
- Harm reduction continuity (needle exchange, OST)
- Safe spaces during conflict
- Legal/policy protections

**Supply Chain Resilience:**
- Buffer stocks at facility level
- Alternative supply routes
- Regional stockpiles
- Emergency procurement mechanisms

**TB Service Continuity:**

**Essential Services:**
- TB treatment continuation (DOT adaptation)
- Contact tracing (may be modified)
- Drug-resistant TB management
- TB preventive therapy

**Adaptations:**
- Video-observed therapy (VOT) vs. in-person DOT
- Home-based medication delivery
- Community treatment supporters
- Monthly supply provision

#### 5.3 Pandemic Planning Assumptions

**WHO Guidance:**
- Assume worst-case scenario (high transmission, severe disease)
- Plan for 25-40% staff absenteeism during peak
- Expect 50-200% surge in healthcare demand
- Anticipate supply chain disruptions
- Assume prolonged duration (months to years)

**Trigger Points for Plan Activation:**

**Alert Level 1 (Preparedness):**
- International outbreak declared
- No local cases
- Actions: Review plans, stockpile, train

**Alert Level 2 (Initial Response):**
- First local cases detected
- Limited community transmission
- Actions: Activate IPC, enhance surveillance, begin modifications

**Alert Level 3 (Sustained Response):**
- Widespread community transmission
- Healthcare surge beginning
- Actions: Full plan activation, alternative sites, service prioritization

**Alert Level 4 (Crisis):**
- Overwhelming surge
- Critical resource shortages
- Actions: Maximum adaptation, mutual aid activation, triage protocols

### FLOW 6: Conflict/Emergency-Affected Settings

**Purpose:** Maintain health services in settings affected by armed conflict, mass displacement, or humanitarian crises

**WHO/EECA Context:**
> "The war in Ukraine has affected not only Ukraine itself but the entire region. Waves of refugees are putting pressure on the capacities and economies of countries that have already been hit by the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic."

#### 6.1 Conflict-Specific Disruptions

**Healthcare Infrastructure:**
- Direct facility damage/destruction
- Attacks on healthcare (violations of humanitarian law)
- Loss of utilities (power, water, communications)
- Blocked access routes

**Healthcare Workforce:**
- Staff casualties
- Staff displacement/migration
- Fear-based absenteeism
- Conscription of medical personnel

**Supply Chains:**
- Border closures
- Transport route destruction
- Supplier displacement
- International sanction effects

**Patient Access:**
- Population displacement
- Insecurity limiting movement
- Destroyed transportation infrastructure
- Psychosocial barriers (fear, trauma)

#### 6.2 Conflict-Specific Continuity Strategies

**Facility Hardening:**
- Backup generators and fuel supplies
- Water storage and purification
- Protective barriers/bunkers
- Emergency communication systems
- Medical supply stockpiles (3-6 months)

**Service Decentralization:**
- Multiple smaller service points vs. centralized hospitals
- Mobile clinics reaching displaced populations
- Community-based care networks
- Cross-border service provision where needed

**Humanitarian Coordination:**
- Integration with humanitarian response mechanisms (OCHA, clusters)
- NGO partnerships for service delivery
- UN agency collaboration (WHO, UNHCR, UNICEF)
- Neutral humanitarian space maintenance

**Refugee/Displaced Population Services:**

**Host Country Continuity Requirements:**
- Surge capacity for increased population
- Expanded services (trauma care, mental health)
- Language/cultural adaptation
- Coordination with refugee services

**Essential Services for Displaced:**
- Emergency care
- Maternal/child health
- Communicable disease control (outbreaks)
- Chronic disease continuity (NCDs, HIV, TB)
- Mental health and psychosocial support
- Sexual and gender-based violence response

#### 6.3 Ukraine War Case Study (from WHO documents)

**Impact:**
- 250,000+ people living with HIV in Ukraine
- 40+ HIV treatment facilities closed
- Service disruption at many remaining sites
- 7.9 million Ukrainians displaced to other countries

**Continuity Responses:**
- Multi-month ART dispensing before displacement
- Cross-border treatment continuation protocols
- Regional coordination for refugee services
- Emergency procurement and distribution
- Telemedicine consultations across borders

**Lessons:**
- Advance preparation critical (stockpiling, MMD)
- Regional cooperation essential
- Flexible policies needed (treatment protocols, drug registration)
- Community networks valuable for maintaining contact with clients

### FLOW 7: Essential Health Service Prioritization

**Purpose:** Systematically determine which services must be maintained during resource-limited emergencies

**WHO Framework:**

#### 7.1 Service Categorization Process

**Step 1: List All Current Services**

Create comprehensive inventory of services provided:
- Outpatient services (by specialty)
- Inpatient services
- Emergency services
- Surgical services
- Diagnostic services (lab, imaging)
- Preventive services
- Public health programs

**Step 2: Apply Prioritization Criteria**

**Criteria for "Essential" Services:**
- Life-saving or life-sustaining
- Prevents significant morbidity if interrupted
- Protects public health (infectious disease control)
- High burden of disease in population
- Vulnerable population needs
- Time-critical (cannot be safely delayed)

**Criteria for "Relocatable" Services:**
- Can be provided at alternative facilities
- Can be delivered through community platforms
- Suitable for telemedicine
- Can be provided by different provider types

**Criteria for "Suspendable" Services:**
- Elective procedures
- Can be safely delayed weeks-months
- Low risk if interrupted briefly
- Non-time-sensitive

**Step 3: Engage Stakeholders**

- Clinical staff input on medical necessity
- Community input on population needs
- Public health authority guidance
- Ethical review (ensure equity)

**Step 4: Document Prioritization**

```
SERVICE PRIORITIZATION MATRIX:

CATEGORY 1: ESSENTIAL - MUST MAINTAIN
├── Service Name
├── Why Essential: (rationale)
├── Resources Required: (minimum)
├── Alternative Delivery Options: (if any)
├── Trigger to Suspend: (if ever applicable)
└── Responsible Team:

CATEGORY 2: IMPORTANT - MAINTAIN IF POSSIBLE
├── Service Name
├── Why Important:
├── Resources Required:
├── Alternative Delivery Options:
├── Trigger to Modify:
└── Responsible Team:

CATEGORY 3: DEFERRABLE - TEMPORARY SUSPENSION OK
├── Service Name
├── Maximum Delay Tolerable:
├── Criteria for Resumption:
└── Communication Plan for Affected Patients:
```

#### 7.2 Context-Specific Prioritization Examples

**Pandemic Context (e.g., COVID-19):**

**Essential (Must Maintain):**
- Emergency care (all causes)
- Critical care
- Maternity services (delivery)
- Dialysis
- Oncology (chemo/radiation)
- HIV ART
- TB treatment
- Mental health crisis

**Important (Maintain with Modifications):**
- Chronic disease management → extended refills, telemedicine
- Routine immunizations → catch-up campaigns later
- Antenatal care → consolidated visits, community-based
- Family planning → pharmacy access, community distribution

**Deferrable (Temporarily Suspend):**
- Elective surgeries → reschedule when capacity available
- Routine screening → delay until surge passes
- Wellness visits → defer to post-emergency

**Conflict Context (e.g., Ukraine):**

**Essential (Must Maintain):**
- Trauma care (conflict-related injuries)
- Emergency obstetric care
- HIV ART (prevent deaths, resist resistance)
- TB treatment (prevent MDR-TB)
- Dialysis
- Insulin for diabetes

**Important (Modify Delivery):**
- Chronic disease care → longer prescriptions, community delivery
- Mental health → group therapy, community counselors
- Immunizations → mobile teams to reach displaced

**Deferrable:**
- Elective procedures
- Routine check-ups
- Specialist consultations (non-urgent)

**Natural Disaster Context (e.g., Earthquake):**

**Essential (Immediate Focus):**
- Trauma surgery (crush injuries, fractures)
- Emergency care (all injuries)
- Obstetric emergencies
- Communicable disease surveillance (outbreak prevention)
- Water/sanitation (prevent epidemics)

**Important (Resume Quickly):**
- Chronic disease management
- Essential medications access
- Immunizations (especially tetanus)

#### 7.3 Quality Maintenance During Prioritization

**WHO Principle:**
> "Even when maintaining the most essential services, quality of care should continue to be assessed using some pragmatic method of performance monitoring."

**Quality Indicators During Emergencies:**
- Patient safety metrics (adverse events, medication errors)
- Infection control compliance
- Essential medicine availability
- Wait times for emergency services
- Mortality rates (overall and cause-specific)
- Patient satisfaction (adapted surveys)

**Minimum Quality Standards:**
- Maintain infection prevention and control
- Medication safety protocols
- Clinical guidelines adherence
- Patient confidentiality
- Informed consent processes
- Respectful care

- Migrants (documented and undocumented)
- Nomadic populations
- Incarcerated persons
- Rural/remote populations
- Racial/ethnic minorities facing discrimination

**Gender-Based Vulnerability:**
- Women and girls (sexual violence, maternal health)
- LGBTQI+ persons
- Sex workers

**Key Populations (HIV/TB Context):**
- People who inject drugs
- Sex workers
- Men who have sex with men
- Transgender persons
- Prisoners

#### 8.2 Vulnerability-Specific Continuity Actions

**Pre-Emergency:**
- Map vulnerable populations in catchment area
- Identify specific health needs
- Establish outreach mechanisms
- Build trust with communities
- Address legal/policy barriers to access

**During Emergency:**
- Proactive outreach (don't wait for them to come)

- Ensure safety and non-discrimination

**Post-Emergency:**
- Assess impact on vulnerable groups

**For Homeless Populations:**

**For Refugees/Displaced Persons:**

**For Rural/Remote Populations:**

### FLOW 9: Healthcare Supply Chain Resilience

**Purpose:** Maintain uninterrupted access to essential medicines, equipment, and supplies during emergencies

**WHO Context:**
> "Essential medical supplies and equipment needs are also identified at the stage of capacity assessment. This usually involves ensuring availability of and access to medicines, medical equipment and supplies that are listed as essential for maintaining basic health services for the population being served."

#### 9.1 Critical Supply Categories

**Life-Sustaining Medications (Cannot Stockpile Long-Term):**
- Antiretroviral drugs (HIV)
- Anti-tuberculosis medications
- Insulin
- Dialysis supplies
- Blood and blood products
- Oxygen
- IV fluids
- Essential chronic disease medications

**Stockpile-Able Supplies:**
- Personal protective equipment (PPE)
- Antibiotics
- Emergency surgical supplies
- Rapid diagnostic tests
- Basic essential medicines
- Syringes and needles
- Wound care supplies

**Emergency-Specific Supplies:**
- Pandemic: PPE, ventilators, oxygen, antivirals
- Conflict: Trauma supplies, blood, surgical kits
- Disaster: Crush syndrome treatment, tetanus vaccine

#### 9.2 Supply Chain Vulnerability Assessment

**Identify Critical Supply Dependencies:**
- Single-source suppliers
- International vs. domestic production
- Long lead times
- Complex cold chains
- Specialized equipment/rare supplies

**Map Supply Chain Routes:**
- Identify chokepoints
- Consider alternative routes
- Assess transportation vulnerabilities
- Understand customs/regulatory delays

**Assess Storage Capacity:**
- Current stock levels
- Storage conditions (temperature, security)
- Inventory management systems
- Expiration tracking

#### 9.3 Supply Continuity Strategies

**Pre-Emergency:**

**Stockpiling:**
- Maintain 3-6 month buffer stocks of critical items
- Rotate stock to prevent expiration
- Secure storage facilities
- Pre-position supplies at alternative sites

**Supplier Diversification:**
- Identify primary, secondary, and emergency suppliers
- Establish pre-approved contracts
- Build relationships with multiple suppliers
- Consider regional vs. international sources

**Emergency Procurement Procedures:**
- Pre-approved emergency procurement authority
- Fast-track approval processes
- Emergency funding mechanisms
- Simplified contracting

**Alternative Supply Sources:**
- National strategic stockpiles
- International emergency supplies (WHO, UNICEF, Global Fund)
- Neighboring facility sharing agreements
- Private pharmacy networks
- Emergency donations (with quality assurance)

**During Emergency:**

**Inventory Management:**
- Real-time stock monitoring
- Prioritize allocation to essential services
- Implement rationing protocols if needed
- Track consumption rates

**Expedited Procurement:**
- Activate emergency procurement
- Use pre-approved suppliers
- Accept alternative formulations/brands if needed
- Fast-track regulatory approvals

**Distribution Adaptations:**
- Alternative transport methods (air, cross-country, etc.)
- Security escorts if needed
- Community distribution points
- Direct delivery to patients (home-based)

**Quality Assurance:**
- Maintain quality standards even in crisis
- Verify emergency donations/purchases
- Proper storage despite constraints
- Monitor for counterfeits

**Post-Emergency:**

- Replenish stocks
- Update supplier contacts
- Document supply chain failures
- Revise procurement procedures

#### 9.4 HIV/TB-Specific Supply Continuity

**HIV ART Continuity:**

**Buffer Stocks:**
- Facility level: 3 months
- Central level: 6 months
- Considerations: ARV regimen diversity

**Multi-Month Dispensing:**

- Enables clients to weather disruptions

**Flexible Procurement:**

- Expedited import approvals

**Alternative Distribution:**
- Pharmacy networks

**TB Drug Continuity:**

**Regional Stockpiles:**

**Treatment Simplification:**

## SECTION 3: FLOWS NOT COVERED IN ISO 22301

The following flows are healthcare-specific and NOT comprehensively addressed in the generic ISO 22301 standard:

### 1. **Clinical Service Prioritization During Resource Scarcity**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 addresses business priority but not life-saving clinical decisions
**Healthcare Requirement:** Systematic triage of services based on medical necessity, vulnerable populations, and ethical principles

### 2. **Healthcare Workforce Unique Vulnerabilities**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 generic workforce planning
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Exposure to infectious diseases (staff as victims and vectors)
- Trauma exposure effects
- Ethical duty to treat vs. personal safety
- Specialized skill requirements (cannot easily replace)

### 3. **Patient Safety and Quality During Continuity**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 focuses on operational continuity, not clinical quality
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Maintain infection prevention standards
- Medication safety during surge
- Ethical triage protocols
- Informed consent under pressure
- Preventing nosocomial infections

### 4. **Medical Supply Chain Criticality**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 generic supply chain
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Life-sustaining medications (cannot run out)
- Cold chain integrity (vaccines, biologics)
- Blood supply continuity
- Oxygen supply
- Specialized medical equipment

### 5. **Vulnerable and Marginalized Population Protections**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 does not address health equity
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Proactive outreach to hard-to-reach populations
- Addressing barriers (financial, language, stigma, legal)
- Culturally appropriate services
- Non-discrimination during triage

### 6. **Disease-Specific Program Continuity (HIV, TB, NCDs)**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 does not address public health programs
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Treatment interruption = death/resistance/transmission
- Complex adherence support needs
- Community-based service delivery
- Peer networks and support groups

### 7. **Clinical Adaptations and Alternative Delivery Models**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 does not cover clinical practice modification
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Telemedicine clinical protocols
- Home-based care clinical guidelines
- Community health worker scope expansion
- Pharmacy-led services
- Modified treatment protocols (e.g., multi-month dispensing)

### 8. **Healthcare Ethics Under Scarcity**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 does not address ethical frameworks
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Triage protocols (who gets scarce resources)
- Informed consent under crisis
- Duty to treat vs. personal safety
- Equitable access during scarcity
- End-of-life care decisions

### 9. **Infection Prevention and Control Integration**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 generic safety measures
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- IPC during infectious disease outbreaks
- Healthcare-associated infection prevention
- Safe clinical waste management during surge
- Environmental decontamination
- PPE protocols for clinical care

### 10. **Public Health Coordination and Reporting**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 internal organizational focus
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Disease surveillance reporting requirements
- Coordination with public health authorities
- Outbreak investigation support
- Vaccination campaign integration
- Cross-border health coordination (refugees)

### 11. **Patient-Centered Communication During Disruption**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 stakeholder communication is general
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Explaining service modifications to patients
- Managing patient expectations during rationing
- Maintaining trust during service disruptions
- Addressing health misinformation
- Culturally sensitive communication

### 12. **Humanitarian Principles in Conflict Settings**
**Gap:** ISO 22301 does not address conflict-specific issues
**Healthcare Requirement:**
- Neutral, impartial, independent humanitarian action
- Protection of healthcare under international humanitarian law
- Attacks on healthcare response
- Serving all sides in conflict
- Cross-line/cross-border service delivery

## SECTION 7: HEALTHCARE-SPECIFIC BCM GLOSSARY

**All-Hazards Approach:** Planning framework addressing common needs across diverse emergencies rather than separate plans for each hazard type.

**After-Action Review (AAR):** Qualitative review of emergency response actions to identify best practices, challenges, and improvements needed.

**Antiretroviral Therapy (ART):** Life-long treatment for HIV; interruption leads to death, resistance, and transmission.

**Business Impact and Risk Analysis (BIRA):** Assessment of disruption sensitivity, time criticality, and interdependencies to prioritize recovery efforts.

**Community Health Worker (CHW):** Trained community member who extends health services into communities; critical for emergency service continuity.

**Differentiated Service Delivery (DSD):** Tailoring service delivery models to patient needs rather than one-size-fits-all (e.g., facility-based, community-based, client-led).

**Essential Health Services:** Life-saving or life-sustaining services that must be maintained during emergencies (e.g., emergency care, maternity, ART, dialysis).

**Health Service Continuity Planning:** Process to maintain continuity of health services routinely provided during potentially disruptive emergencies.

**Hospital Incident Management System (HIMS):** Organizational structure for coordinating hospital emergency response (operations, planning, logistics, administration).

**Infection Prevention and Control (IPC):** Measures to prevent healthcare-associated infections and protect staff/patients from infectious hazards.

**Key Populations:** Groups at higher risk of HIV (people who inject drugs, sex workers, MSM, transgender persons, prisoners) requiring specialized services.

**Multi-Month Dispensing (MMD):** Providing 3-6 months of medication supply to stable patients; critical strategy for treatment continuity during emergencies.

**Mutual Aid Agreement:** Pre-established agreement between facilities to share resources, staff, or patients during emergencies.

**Surge Capacity:** Ability to expand beyond normal capacity to meet increased demand (function of staff, space, supplies, systems).

**Vulnerable Populations:** Groups with extra medical needs or at higher risk of poor outcomes during emergencies (requires special planning considerations).

## SECTION 8: CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTORS

Based on WHO guidance and EECA experience, the following factors are CRITICAL for successful healthcare service continuity:

### 1. **Senior Leadership Commitment**
- Allocate budget for planning
- Approve plan
- Support implementation
- Champion culture of preparedness

### 2. **Inclusive Stakeholder Engagement**
- Internal: All facility departments
- External: Health authorities, partners, community
- Private sector collaboration
- Cross-sectoral coordination

### 3. **Regular Testing and Updating**
- Annual simulation exercises minimum
- Update after exercises and real events
- Continuous risk reassessment
- Staff trained on current version

### 4. **Pre-Positioned Resources**
- Emergency stockpiles (3-6 months critical supplies)
- Pre-approved suppliers and contracts
- Emergency funding mechanisms
- Identified surge staff

### 5. **Flexible and Adaptive Planning**
- All-hazards base + hazard-specific annexes
- Alternative delivery models pre-identified
- Clear adaptation procedures
- Learning culture (AAR + plan updates)

### 6. **Community Partnership**
- Community involvement in planning
- CHW programs established
- Trust built before emergencies
- Alternative delivery through community

### 7. **Equity at Center**
- Vulnerable populations explicitly considered
- Proactive outreach mechanisms
- Barriers addressed (financial, language, stigma)
- Non-discrimination during triage

### 8. **Integration with National Systems**
- Aligned with national emergency plans
- Participate in coordination mechanisms
- Access to national resources
- Coherent reporting systems

## CONCLUSION

WHO healthcare business continuity guidance provides actionable frameworks for maintaining essential health services during diverse emergencies. Key differentiators from generic ISO 22301 BCM include:

1. **Patient-Centered Focus:** Prioritizing life-saving/sustaining services, not just business operations
2. **Clinical Quality Imperative:** Maintaining care quality even when services modified
3. **Vulnerable Population Protection:** Explicit equity focus, proactive outreach
4. **Disease Program Continuity:** Specialized approaches for HIV, TB, NCDs
5. **Healthcare Workforce Vulnerabilities:** Exposure risks, specialized skills, ethical duties
6. **Medical Supply Criticality:** Life-sustaining medications that cannot be interrupted
7. **Alternative Delivery Models:** Community-based, telemedicine, peer-led services
8. **Public Health Integration:** Coordination with disease surveillance, outbreak response
9. **Humanitarian Principles:** Neutral, impartial care in conflict settings
10. **All-Hazards Flexibility:** Unified planning for multiple emergency types

**Implementation Priority:**
Organizations implementing healthcare BCM should start with the 8-step planning lifecycle (Flow 1), ensure all health system building blocks are addressed (Flow 3), and adopt healthcare-specific best practices (Section 4), particularly multi-month dispensing, differentiated service delivery, and community health worker integration.

**Continuous Improvement:**
Service continuity planning is not a one-time exercise but an ongoing cycle of planning, testing, implementing, reviewing, and improving. Organizations should institutionalize after-action reviews and apply the "Build Back Better" principle to strengthen health system resilience over time.
