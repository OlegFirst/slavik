Real Conversation Example: BIA Specialist

See how the BIA Specialist provides tactical guidance for a medium healthcare organization

User (BCM Manager at 500-bed hospital):

"I'm starting a BIA for our hospital. How do I identify which functions are critical? We have emergency services, surgeries, labs, radiology, ICU, and administrative departments."

BIA Specialist:

Great question! For a medium healthcare organization like yours, here's the structured approach to identify critical functions:

1. Apply Healthcare-Specific Criteria (ISO 22301 Clause 8.2.2)

  • Patient Safety Impact: Functions where disruption causes immediate patient harm
  • Regulatory Requirements: Functions mandated by healthcare regulations
  • Revenue Impact: Functions generating significant revenue or incurring high costs
  • Reputation Risk: Functions where failure damages trust

2. Priority Classification for Your Hospital

Function Priority Typical RTO Rationale
Emergency Services Critical (1) 0 hours Life-threatening delays
ICU Critical (1) 0 hours Continuous patient monitoring
Surgeries Critical (1) 4 hours Urgent procedures only
Lab Services Important (2) 8-24 hours Diagnostic support
Radiology Important (2) 8-24 hours Imaging support
Administrative Standard (3) 48-72 hours Non-clinical support

3. Validation Steps

  1. Stakeholder Interviews: Meet with department heads (Emergency Director, Chief Nursing Officer, Lab Manager)
  2. Regulatory Check: Verify against Joint Commission / CMS requirements
  3. Historical Review: Analyze past incidents (power outages, IT failures) to see actual impacts
  4. Executive Approval: Present classification to leadership for validation

4. Next Steps for You

  • Start with Emergency Services + ICU (highest risk)
  • Use BIA template: [Link to healthcare BIA template]
  • Schedule interviews in this order: ER → ICU → Surgery → Labs
  • Expected timeline: 3-4 weeks for initial BIA

Similar Organizations (Benchmarking):
